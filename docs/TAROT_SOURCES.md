# Tarot Interpretation Sources

The reading system is designed around the Rider-Waite-Smith tradition, with Arthur Edward Waite's *The Pictorial Key to the Tarot* (1911) as the primary historical source.

## Primary source

Arthur Edward Waite, *The Pictorial Key to the Tarot*, illustrated by Pamela Colman Smith (1911).

Public-domain text:
https://www.sacred-texts.com/tarot/pkt/index.htm

The book contains Waite's discussion of the Greater and Lesser Arcana, divinatory meanings, the practical art of Tarot divination, and the published Celtic Cross method.

### Celtic Cross convention used by the application

The ten positions follow Waite's published functions:

1. What Covers — the general influence or atmosphere affecting the matter.
2. What Crosses — the obstacle or opposing force.
3. What Crowns — the querent's aim or ideal, and the best that can be achieved under the circumstances.
4. What Is Beneath — the foundation or basis already established.
5. What Is Behind — the influence just passed or passing away.
6. What Is Before — the influence coming into action in the near future.
7. Himself / Herself — the querent's position or attitude.
8. The House / Environment — surrounding people, conditions, and tendencies affecting the matter.
9. Hopes or Fears — hopes and fears invested in the matter.
10. What Will Come — the final result or culmination produced by the other influences.

The original Waite method also selected a separate Significator before the ten-card operation. The application currently keeps the user experience to a ten-card draw while retaining Waite's ten position functions.

## Five-card spreads

There is no single universally canonical five-card spread. The application therefore offers several established five-card patterns and clearly labels them as choices rather than claiming that one layout is the definitive traditional five-card spread.

The available choices are:

- Situation & Advice
- Five-Card Cross
- Expanded Past–Present–Future
- Relationship Cross
- Decision / Crossroads
- Timeline

These are intended as practical, recognizable spread variants. Their position meanings are written in modern language while preserving the purpose of the layout.

## Interpretation method

The application does not copy Waite's prose into the user interface. Instead, it uses concise modern card themes derived from the historical source and combines them with the function of the selected spread position. This produces a contextual interpretation: the same card can therefore mean something different when it appears in the Past, Challenge, Guidance, Relationship, or Outcome position.

Reversed cards are treated as a variation of the same core archetype: blocked, delayed, internalized, exaggerated, or difficult expression of the upright theme.
